# Heroes and Equipment

Heroes should be nearly as simple and readable as creature cards in a collectible card game.

A hero is not intended to function as a traditional RPG character sheet.

The hero card should communicate:

* recruitment cost
* class
* core strengths
* areas of specialization
* acquired advantages
* persistent complications

Most long-term build complexity should come from the guild and the relationships between cards rather than from adding more statistics to individual heroes.

---

# Hero Card Structure

A hero card uses the following visual hierarchy:

1. Recruitment Cost
2. Hero Name
3. Portrait
4. Class
5. Core Statistics and Specializations
6. Enhancements
7. Drawbacks

Recruitment Cost appears in the upper corner of the card, occupying the position normally used for mana cost in a collectible card game.

The center of the card is dominated by the hero's portrait.

The rules area beneath the portrait contains:

> **Class**
>
> **Capability # — Specialization**
> **Control # — Specialization**
> **Stamina # — Specialization**
>
> **Enhancements**
> **Drawbacks**

This structure should allow the player to understand the broad value of a hero at a glance.

---

# Core Hero Statistics

Every hero has three core statistics.

## Capability

Capability measures the hero's ability to make progress and actively solve problems.

Depending on the situation, Capability may represent:

* physical force
* speed
* magical power
* technical knowledge
* persuasion
* leadership
* medical skill

Capability describes how much the hero can accomplish.

---

## Control

Control measures the hero's ability to manage danger, uncertainty, and instability.

Depending on the situation, Control may represent:

* physical defense
* precision
* awareness
* restraint
* magical containment
* tactical discipline
* social composure

Control describes how well the hero prevents a situation from deteriorating.

---

## Stamina

Stamina measures the hero's ability to continue functioning under pressure.

Depending on the situation, Stamina may represent:

* physical endurance
* mental resolve
* courage
* patience
* resistance to corruption
* resistance to exhaustion
* general expedition readiness

Stamina describes how much strain the hero can endure before becoming unable to continue.

---

# Contextual Interpretation

The three statistics remain mechanically consistent across different kinds of encounters.

Their narrative meaning changes according to the situation.

During combat:

* Capability may represent attacks and offensive action.
* Control may represent defense and positioning.
* Stamina may represent health and endurance.

During exploration:

* Capability may represent clearing obstacles or making progress.
* Control may represent navigation and hazard management.
* Stamina may represent fatigue and exposure.

During negotiation:

* Capability may represent persuasion or leadership.
* Control may represent composure and reading the opposition.
* Stamina may represent patience, confidence, and emotional resilience.

Heroes therefore use a stable mechanical language even when encounters present very different problems.

The detailed encounter system remains to be determined.

---

# Specializations

Each core statistic has a branching tree of specializations.

Specializations describe how a hero applies their statistics.

They should not become additional numerical attributes.

The statistic describes **how much** a hero can accomplish.

The specialization describes **how** they accomplish it.

Specialization trees are limited to a depth of two:

1. Broad Specialization
2. Focused Specialization

A hero may possess only a broad specialization or may possess both a broad and focused specialization.

Example:

> **Capability 4 — Athletic: Strong**

In this example:

* Capability is the core statistic.
* Athletic is the broad specialization.
* Strong is the focused specialization.

A hero could instead have:

> **Capability 4 — Athletic**

This hero is broadly athletic but has not developed or acquired a more focused identity within that branch.

---

# Initial Capability Specializations

Capability represents active progress and problem-solving.

## Athletic

Physical action, movement, and bodily performance.

Focused specializations:

* Strong
* Fast

---

## Arcana

Interaction with supernatural forces, rituals, and metaphysical knowledge.

Focused specializations:

* Magic
* Faith

---

## Charisma

The ability to influence, inspire, persuade, or captivate others.

Focused specializations:

* Leadership
* Charm

---

Additional Capability branches may later include areas such as Expertise, Craft, Lore, or Medicine.

These should only be introduced when the game contains enough relevant decisions to support them.

---

# Control and Stamina Specializations

Control and Stamina will use the same two-depth structure as Capability.

Their final specialization trees remain to be designed.

Potential Control concepts include:

* Awareness
* Precision
* Discipline
* Protection

Potential Stamina concepts include:

* Body
* Mind
* Spirit
* Adaptation

These are provisional directions rather than finalized branches.

The initial prototype should include only enough specializations to test whether they meaningfully change hero valuation and expedition composition.

---

# Class

Every hero has a Class.

Class provides a recognizable fantasy identity and may eventually influence:

* equipment interactions
* available Enhancements
* recruitment markets
* training opportunities
* party composition
* guild doctrines

Class should initially remain a light mechanical tag rather than a complete rules package.

The prototype includes only one Hero class.

Additional classes should not be introduced until the core statistic and specialization system has been validated.

---

# Enhancements

Enhancements are persistent positive changes acquired by a hero.

A hero may have no more than two Enhancements.

Enhancements may represent:

* specialized training
* battlefield experience
* magical blessings
* mutations
* equipment mastery
* guild honors
* exposure to relics
* meaningful relationships
* unusual discoveries

Enhancements belong to the hero rather than to their equipment.

They remain with the hero when equipment is reassigned.

Examples:

> **Caravan Veteran**
> Gains additional Control during escort contracts.

> **Fog-Touched**
> May use Arcana when interacting with the Deep Fog.

> **Natural Leader**
> Leadership benefits other heroes in the expedition.

Enhancements should ideally change how the player values or uses the hero rather than merely adding a universal numerical bonus.

---

# Drawbacks

Drawbacks are persistent complications carried by a hero.

A hero may have no more than two Drawbacks.

Drawbacks replace the narrower category of Injuries.

An injury may be a Drawback, but not every Drawback is an injury.

Drawbacks may represent:

* physical injuries
* illness
* trauma
* destructive habits
* personality conflicts
* debts
* criminal history
* damaged reputation
* supernatural corruption
* difficult obligations

Examples:

> **Crushed Hand**
> Strong cannot be applied to tasks requiring both hands.

> **Fog-Sick**
> Suffers additional strain during Deep expeditions.

> **Wanted**
> Creates risk during contracts involving authorities.

> **Arrogant**
> Resists support from lower-level heroes.

Drawbacks are not intended to make heroes objectively worthless.

They should create different valuations between guilds.

A drawback that makes a hero unacceptable to one guild may create an opportunity for another.

This is particularly important to the identity of the Rehab Guild, which recognizes value in heroes others consider damaged, unreliable, or difficult.

---

# Equipment Cards

Equipment exists as separate cards rather than printed features on hero cards.

Equipment functions similarly to artifact cards in a collectible card game.

The guild owns Equipment independently of the heroes who use it.

Equipment can therefore be:

* purchased
* stored
* assigned
* reassigned
* improved
* lost
* damaged
* built around by guild systems

Separating heroes from Equipment reinforces the distinction between personnel and institutional resources.

---

# Generic Equipment Capacity

Each hero has two generic Equipment slots.

Equipment slots are not divided into fixed categories such as Weapon and Armor.

Any legal combination of two Equipment cards may be assigned to a hero.

Examples include:

* two Daggers
* Sword and Shield
* Weapon and Armor
* Armor and Tool
* two magical Foci
* two Tools
* Banner and Shield

This allows equipment combinations to express the hero's current role.

A Thief equipped with two Daggers is configured aggressively.

That same Thief may later exchange one Dagger for Light Armor when the expedition becomes more dangerous.

Equipment restrictions, when necessary, should appear on the individual Equipment cards.

Examples may eventually include:

* Two-Handed
* Bulky
* Off-Hand
* Class requirements
* Specialization requirements

A piece of Equipment may occupy both slots if its rules specify that it does so.

---

# Equipment State

Equipment uses tapped and untapped states.

## Tapped Equipment

Tapped Equipment is assigned to a hero and currently equipped.

Each tapped Equipment card occupies one of that hero's Equipment slots unless otherwise stated.

## Untapped Equipment

Untapped Equipment is available within the expedition's Equipment pool.

It is not currently assigned to a hero and provides no benefit until tapped.

The tapped state therefore represents commitment rather than temporary exhaustion.

---

# Expedition Equipment Pool

An expedition may bring up to ten Equipment cards.

These cards form the expedition's Equipment pool.

The guild may own more Equipment than the expedition can carry.

This creates three distinct layers of equipment management:

## Guild Inventory

All Equipment currently owned by the guild.

## Expedition Pool

Up to ten Equipment cards selected to accompany the expedition.

## Hero Loadout

Up to two Equipment cards currently tapped to a specific hero.

The expedition pool limits logistical flexibility without requiring a detailed inventory simulation.

---

# Equipment During Activation

Once during each hero Activation, that hero may:

* untap up to one Equipment card currently assigned to them
* tap up to one available Equipment card from the expedition pool to themselves

These are separate allowances.

A hero may therefore exchange one piece of Equipment during an Activation.

Example:

1. Untap one Dagger.
2. Tap one Light Armor.

The hero cannot replace their entire loadout during a single Activation.

Equipment cannot be transferred directly from one hero to another while it remains tapped.

It must first be untapped and returned to the expedition pool.

---

# Initial Loadouts

At the beginning of an expedition, each hero enters with a default loadout of up to two Equipment cards already tapped to them.

These initial assignments do not count against the hero's first Activation.

The default loadout represents the equipment selected and prepared before the expedition begins.

Once the expedition is underway, normal Equipment rules apply.

---

# Equipment Design

Basic Equipment may provide direct bonuses to one of the three core statistics.

Examples:

> **Iron Sword**
> +1 Capability

> **Light Armor**
> +1 Control

> **Expedition Pack**
> +1 Stamina

More advanced Equipment should interact with specializations, classes, Enhancements, Drawbacks, or guild systems.

Examples:

> **Balanced Dagger**
> Improves Capability when Athletic: Fast is relevant.

> **Reliquary Censer**
> Improves Control when Faith is used to contain supernatural danger.

> **Captain's Standard**
> Allows Leadership to support additional heroes.

Equipment should create loadout decisions rather than merely form a ladder of increasingly large bonuses.

The player should ask:

> "Which equipment combination best supports this hero and this expedition?"

rather than:

> "Which item has the highest number?"

---

# Current Hero Model

The current hero model consists of:

* Recruitment Cost
* Hero Name
* Portrait
* Class
* Capability
* Control
* Stamina
* One specialization line for each core statistic
* Up to two Enhancements
* Up to two Drawbacks
* Two generic Equipment slots

An expedition may bring up to ten Equipment cards.

Each hero begins with a default loadout and may exchange one equipped item during each Activation.

This model is intended to produce heroes that are immediately readable while still allowing meaningful differences in identity, history, and use.

The hero card remains simple.

The guild determines what that hero can become.
