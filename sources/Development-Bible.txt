# Phantom Company

## Design Bible v0.2

### Minimum Viable Fun & Product Vision

---

# Vision

**Phantom Company** is a guild management RPG set in a dungeonpunk fantasy world where players build an adventuring guild rather than a single hero.

The player is not an adventurer.

The player is the institution behind the adventurers.

Heroes come and go.

Guilds endure.

Success is measured not by assembling the strongest party, but by building an organization whose philosophy allows it to recognize opportunities that other guilds overlook.

---

# Setting

Centuries ago, explorers discovered an impossible chasm beneath the world.

Within it drifted a supernatural mist known as **The Deep Fog**.

The deeper one travels into the Fog, the stranger reality becomes.

Entire ecosystems exist within its layers.

Ancient civilizations, forgotten ruins, impossible monsters, and miraculous artifacts lie hidden below.

The first guild to descend into the Deep Fog never returned.

Known today only as **The Phantom Company**, its members began appearing throughout later expeditions.

Sometimes they guided lost explorers.

Sometimes they rescued doomed parties.

Sometimes they slaughtered entire guilds without explanation.

No one agrees whether they are alive, dead, cursed, immortal, or something stranger.

Every guild entering the Deep Fog hopes to uncover the truth.

Every guild believes they will succeed where the Phantom Company failed.

---

# The Guild Economy

Civilization has reorganized around exploration.

Settlements exist along the stable edge of the first layer of the Deep Fog.

Guilds recruit adventurers, fulfill contracts, recover relics, map new routes, rescue lost expeditions, and trade discoveries.

A guild's prestige is measured by **Guild Level**.

Guild Level is not experience.

It represents the deepest verified layer of the Deep Fog successfully explored by that guild.

Higher Guild Levels unlock:

* deeper expeditions
* greater prestige
* better recruits
* more influential contracts
* access to increasingly valuable relics

The deepest layers remain largely unknown.

---

# Core Design Philosophy

## The Guild Is the Build

The guild is the player's primary character.

Heroes are valuable assets.

Facilities, doctrines, staff, relics, and reputation are the player's long-term progression.

---

## Everything Has Value

Nothing should be universally good or bad.

The same hero, relic, mission, or contract should have different value depending on the guild's philosophy.

A broken veteran may be worthless to one guild and priceless to another.

---

## Buildcraft Over Optimization

Players should constantly ask:

> "What if I built around this?"

rather than

> "Which number is biggest?"

The most exciting upgrades should bend rules rather than increase statistics.

---

## Institutions Shape Heroes

Heroes become exceptional because of the guild that trains, equips, supports, and believes in them.

The game tells stories about organizations.

---

# Minimum Viable Fun

The first playable prototype must answer one question:

> Do different guild philosophies create different decisions when presented with the same heroes and opportunities?

If three guilds consistently value identical markets differently, the core vision is validated.

---

# Core Gameplay Loop

1. Review available heroes, equipment, and contracts.
2. Expand and customize the guild.
3. Assemble an expedition.
4. Resolve an encounter.
5. Return with rewards, injuries, and discoveries.
6. Invest in the guild.
7. Repeat.

---

# Guild Progression

Every guild is composed of persistent organizational elements.

## Heroes

Temporary assets.

Heroes level, gain experience, suffer injuries, develop loyalty, and eventually retire or perish.

Heroes are intentionally replaceable.

The guild is not.

---

## Facilities

Facilities define what a guild is capable of doing.

Facilities function as **Doctrine Sockets**.

Initial prototype facilities:

* Barracks
* Trophy Hall
* Hospital

Every guild can construct every facility.

Each guild specialization enhances one facility.

---

## Doctrines

Doctrines are the operational philosophy of the guild.

Rather than passive bonuses, doctrines establish rules under which the guild operates.

They function similarly to build-defining skill gems.

Prototype doctrines:

* Regimen
* Party
* Succor

The same doctrine may behave differently depending on which facility it occupies.

---

## Staff

Staff are permanent professionals employed by the guild.

They do not participate directly in expeditions.

Instead they modify doctrines and facilities.

Prototype staff:

* Veteran
* Quartermaster
* Chirurgeon

---

## Relics

Relics represent the history and culture of the guild.

Prototype relics:

* Statue
* Skull
* Banner

Relics further modify the effects of doctrines and facilities.

---

# Organizational Resources

## Gold

Operational currency.

Used to recruit heroes, purchase equipment, heal injuries, and improve the guild.

---

## Reputation

Public perception.

Reputation is generally not spent.

Instead it unlocks:

* higher quality recruits
* prestigious contracts
* influential patrons
* rare opportunities

Future guild types, such as Diplomacy, may deliberately risk or leverage Reputation.

---

## Renown

Internal organizational prestige.

Renown is earned through accomplishments.

Renown unlocks:

* new doctrines
* organizational traits
* facility improvements
* deeper specialization

---

# Initial Guild Specializations

## Training Guild

Core belief:

Potential is undervalued.

Primary Facility:

Barracks

Primary axis manipulated:

Potential

---

## Raiding Guild

Core belief:

Immediate strength creates opportunity.

Primary Facility:

Trophy Hall

Primary axis manipulated:

Risk

---

## Rehab Guild

Core belief:

Broken heroes are overlooked assets.

Primary Facility:

Hospital

Primary axis manipulated:

Time

---

# Future Guild Philosophies

Future guild identities should manipulate unique axes of the game rather than simply generating more resources.

Examples include:

Crafting → Items

Diplomacy → Reputation

Exploration → Information

Future specializations should continue expanding this design philosophy.

---

# Technical MVF Scope

Prototype with:

* Three guild specializations
* One Hero class
* Static hero market
* Simple equipment
* Single abstract encounter system
* Three facilities
* Three doctrines
* Three staff types
* Three relic types
* Gold
* Reputation
* Renown

Explicitly excluded:

* Hero classes
* Player trading
* Tactical grid combat
* Crafting
* Complex dungeon generation
* Multiplayer economy
* Seasonal play
* Hero transfer between guilds
* Resurrection systems

These systems should only be introduced after the core gameplay thesis has been validated.

---

# Design Principles

Every permanent progression system should answer at least one question:

* What rule does this change?
* What decision does this create?
* How does this change what the player values?

If a feature does none of these, it should be reconsidered.

The objective is not to create the strongest guild.

The objective is to create a guild whose philosophy changes how the player sees the world.
